Add a message format to the MS Teams channel, and the Button block for its Adaptive card format.

The Teams channel node gains `message_format: "text" | "adaptive_card"`. A template without it is "text", which is how Teams messages are delivered today: markdown, with any action as a link. "adaptive_card" sends one Adaptive Card with the text and a row of buttons. Hosts switch it with `useMSTeamsFormat`, which also reports why a switch is blocked. A format the template's blocks can't show is disabled with the reason: buttons block Text, and dividers, quotes and headings block Adaptive card.

In an Adaptive card a button without a link is flagged as a warning in the issue gutter (`msteams-button-no-link`) and drawn with a dotted border, since Teams only sends buttons that open a link. Each format greys out the sidebar blocks it can't show, with a tooltip on how to enable them: Button in Text, Divider in Adaptive card. In Adaptive card the quote and heading options are disabled in the text menu, a quote or heading made any other way (the `> ` and `# ` input rules, Mod-Shift-b) becomes a plain paragraph (`MSTeamsCardTextOnly`), dividers and images are dropped however they arrive (paste, a dropped image file, the `---` rule), images also block switching into it, and buttons stay in one row after every other block — the document keeps them last, so a drop lands where its indicator shows. In Text, a button already in the template is drawn as the link it is delivered as, and pasted buttons are dropped. Teams buttons use the label + link form, with bold/italic hidden on the label.

Teams buttons are dragged by the whole button rather than a grip, selected with a ring that hugs the button, with their duplicate/delete toolbar below it; their label and link (with its link-tracking switch) are edited only in the sidebar (the button's own text isn't editable in place). Dragging a button along the row drops it before or after the hovered button, by the half under the pointer, and the row previews the new order while dragging (the dragged button leaves its slot and a ghost with its label, sized like a button, takes the new one); dragged off the row it stays where it was, and a new button from the sidebar dropped anywhere else in the message ends the row. In an Adaptive card, pasted quotes become plain text (a quote inside a quote too), and `MessagingChannelPaste` gains `unwrapNodeTypes` for this. Reordering the row keeps the caret in the block it was in. `DropRules` gains `wholeDragTypes` for this, and `trailingTypes` (block types kept in a trailing row; a preset such as `button:primary` counts as its block type) and `setTrailingTypes`. The MS Teams frame now looks like a current Teams channel post, with a skeleton reply-in-thread footer. New exports: `useMSTeamsFormat`, `getMSTeamsFormat`, `useMSTeamsSidebarHasDetails`; `MSTeamsRenderProps` gains an optional `setSelectedNode`. In an Adaptive card the row shows the first 6 buttons, as Teams does, and the rest under a "…" chip. Its menu selects a button (with its duplicate and delete; read-only when the editor isn't editable, even without the `readOnly` prop), reorders them by dragging, and drags one into the row (read-only, it only lists them); a button dropped on the chip goes under it, and holding a drag over the chip opens the menu to drop it at a place. Where a dragged button lands is read off the row laid out without its ghost, as is the "…" chip's place (the chip takes a drop only there), and the ghost is a row item of its own, so it shows the line the button lands on and doesn't flicker as it moves the row. `DropRules` gains `layoutTrailingDrop` and `currentSourceId` for this.
